BC08 Hybrid and multicloud estates

Multicloud data optimization

Move training data between clouds by what the workload needs, not by the terabyte.

Generate workload-specific optimized datasets before authorized transfers between private infrastructure and cloud platforms, reducing duplication and transfer of large datasets.

Business problem

AI workloads span private infrastructure and several clouds, forcing costly duplication and transfer of large training datasets.

Proposed solution

Distill at the source environment and transfer only the workload-specific dataset to the target platform.

Primary outcome

Less data duplicated and moved between environments for AI workloads.

01Business challenge

Enterprise data estates now span on-premises data centers, private clouds and several public cloud platforms. Data is often generated or held in one environment while the AI services, accelerators or teams that need it sit in another.

The conventional answer is to replicate complete datasets to wherever training runs. Each copy incurs transfer and egress charges, ingestion and storage in the target platform, and another dataset to secure and keep in sync.

As AI workloads move between providers for capacity, price or capability, the cost and complexity of moving data becomes a constraint on architectural choice.

02Why existing approaches are insufficient

Replicate complete datasets to each platform

Simplest for data science teams but multiplies egress, storage and synchronization effort for every copy.

Query in place across clouds

Avoids bulk copies for analytics, but training workloads read data repeatedly and cross-cloud reads can be slow and charged per access.

Consolidate on a single platform

Removes cross-cloud movement but is a long migration program and reduces flexibility to use capacity or services from other providers.

Generic compression and deduplication

Reduces bytes on the wire but is task-agnostic; most of the original volume still moves and must be stored.

Trade-offs describe generic architectural approaches, not specific products.

03GroundTruth AI solution

GroundTruth AI inserts a distillation step before authorized transfers. In the environment where the data resides, the engine produces a workload-specific training dataset and fidelity report. Only that dataset is transferred to the target platform, through the customer's existing transfer tooling. The full dataset stays in its home environment.

  1. Select the workload

    Identify a training workload that runs in a different environment from its data.

  2. Distill at the source

    Run distillation in the source environment with customer-defined accuracy tolerances.

  3. Validate

    Compare models trained on full and distilled data on the same held-out test set.

  4. Transfer the distilled dataset

    Move only the approved dataset to the target platform through existing, authorized tooling.

  5. Train and regenerate

    Train on the target platform; regenerate the dataset when the source data or task changes.

What this does not do

  • It does not replace data integration, synchronization or master-data management between platforms.
  • It does not change provider pricing; savings depend on the customer's actual transfer, storage and processing rates.
  • Distilled datasets remain subject to the same security, residency and contractual controls as their source.

04Reference architecture

  • Raw data
  • Distilled dataset
  • Model / results
  • Control / approvals
  • GroundTruth AI component
  • Distillation runs where the data resides, so its compute is billed in the source environment and must be included in the net calculation.
  • The full dataset remains the system of record in its home environment.

05Business outcomes

Direct cost reduction

  • Lower egress and interconnect charges for eligible training transfers.
  • Less ingestion and storage in the target platform.
  • Lower processing of training data in the target platform, net of source-side distillation cost.

Operational efficiency

  • Fewer full dataset replicas to secure and keep in sync.
  • Faster provisioning of training data on a new platform.
  • Simpler data lifecycle for cross-platform AI workloads.

Strategic enablement

  • More freedom to place training workloads where capacity or services are best suited.
  • Reduced dependence on large-scale migration programs for AI use cases.
  • A consistent pattern for distributed AI infrastructure across providers.

Strategic value is assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of each cost line and are net of the cost of distillation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Implementation approach

For a pilot, distillation runs as a batch job in the source environment on exports of one workload's training data. Native connectors for cloud storage and data platforms are proposed roadmap items.

Required infrastructure

  • Compute in the source environment for distillation jobs.
  • Read access to the source dataset for the selected workload.
  • The existing authorized transfer path to the target platform.

Operational constraints

  • Savings depend on the customer's negotiated transfer, storage and compute rates.
  • Workloads that need the full dataset, such as exploratory analysis, are out of scope.
  • Scaling to enterprise data volumes has not yet been benchmarked.

09Evidence

Proposed enterprise application — not yet validated in production

Internal experiments show large data reductions with limited accuracy change on public benchmarks. The effect on intercloud transfer, storage and processing costs has not yet been measured on a customer estate.

What supports it

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).

What is not yet validated

  • No measurement of transfer or storage cost on customer multicloud infrastructure.
  • No benchmark at enterprise data volumes.
  • No native connectors for cloud storage or data platforms built yet.
  • No production deployment.

Risks we manage in the pilot

  • Distillation compute in the source environment may offset transfer savings for infrequent transfers.
  • Workloads may require regeneration more often than expected if source data changes rapidly.

10Pilot proposal

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One training workload whose data is replicated from its home environment to another platform.
Required inputs
  • The source dataset and the current replication process
  • Current transfer, storage and processing costs for the workload
  • The training pipeline and evaluation metric on the target platform
  • Compute in the source environment
Existing baseline
The current workload trained on the full replicated dataset.
Evaluation methodology
  • Train on full and distilled data; evaluate both on the same held-out test set.
  • Measure transfer volume, egress cost, target storage and processing for both paths.
  • Measure source-side distillation compute and cost.
Technical success criteria
  • Model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline.
  • Distilled datasets transfer through existing tooling without changes to controls.
Economic success criteria
  • Transfer and target-platform storage reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Net saving positive after source-side overhead at the customer's transfer frequency.
Deliverables
  • Fidelity report
  • Transfer, storage, processing and overhead comparison at the customer's rates
  • Assessment of other eligible cross-platform workloads
  • Production adoption plan
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • Security and data owner approval
  • Agreed regeneration policy
