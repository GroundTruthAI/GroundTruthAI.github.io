01Business challenge

Distributed sensors, industrial devices, network equipment, vehicles and cameras produce continuous data streams. In many estates the volume generated at the edge already exceeds what can be transmitted, ingested, stored or processed centrally at an acceptable cost, particularly where connectivity is cellular, satellite or shared with operational traffic.

Central AI teams still need representative training data from these fleets. The usual answer is to ship as much as the link allows and sort it out in the cloud, which turns bandwidth, ingestion fees and central storage into a cost that grows with every new device.

The decision of what to keep is therefore made by link capacity and fixed retention rules, not by what a given model actually needs to learn.

02Business opportunity

Less network traffic and cloud ingestion for AI training, with a scalable distributed architecture.

Where conventional architectures fall short

Transmit everything and filter centrally

Simple to operate, but bandwidth, ingestion and central storage scale with raw volume, and constrained links force data to be dropped or delayed without regard to its value.

Fixed sampling, compression or aggregation at the edge

Predictable and lightweight, but task-agnostic: generic compression keeps redundancy and fixed sampling or averaging can discard the rare events a model must learn from.

Event-triggered capture rules

Useful for known conditions, but rules must be maintained by hand for every device type and miss patterns nobody anticipated.

Run full model training at the edge

Avoids transfer but requires training-capable hardware and ML operations at every site, and fragments model development across the fleet.

Generic architectural approaches, not specific products. How distillation compares with other techniques.

03GroundTruth AI approach

GroundTruth AI places the data-reduction step near the point of generation. A distillation runtime on an edge gateway or site server builds a compact, task-specific training dataset from locally buffered data, validates it, and forwards only the approved distilled dataset through the existing uplink. Raw data stays at the edge under local retention rules.

  1. Define the task

    Central teams specify the model, the variables and the acceptance thresholds for accuracy and event coverage.

  2. Buffer locally

    Edge data is collected in a local buffer on the gateway or site server, as many estates already do for store-and-forward.

  3. Distill near the source

    The runtime generates a distilled dataset for the task from each batch of buffered data, on a schedule set by the customer.

  4. Validate and forward

    A fidelity summary accompanies each dataset; approved datasets are sent through the existing uplink and ingestion pipeline.

  5. Train centrally

    Central teams train on aggregated distilled datasets and adjust the task definition when requirements change.

What this does not do

  • It does not claim real-time or streaming execution; distillation runs on batches at a cadence set by the customer.
  • It does not imply compatibility with any specific edge device; target hardware must be qualified during the pilot.
  • It does not replace edge security, device management or network design.

04Illustrative enterprise scenario

Illustrative enterprise scenario Hypothetical organization; assumptions are for discussion and not contractual.

Telecommunications operator

Enterprise context
A telecommunications operator runs thousands of network sites that generate telemetry, performance counters, alarms and operational events.
Current architecture
Telemetry is aggregated regionally and shipped to central platforms. Much of it is stored only to feed periodic model training.
Business problem
The operator wants centralized anomaly-detection and network-performance models. Moving raw telemetry from every region consumes transport capacity, ingestion pipelines and central storage, and most of the volume is routine.
Proposed GroundTruth AI architecture
Distillation runs in regional data centers or aggregation points. It produces task-specific training datasets that keep unusual network events and the temporal context around them. Only those datasets travel to the central platform for training.

Illustrative assumptions

  • 12 regions × 50 TB of eligible telemetry per training cycle = 600 TB
  • Assumed dataset reduction: 85%, an illustrative planning value
  • Optimized data transferred: 90 TB
  • Potential avoided transfer: 510 TB per training cycle

Planning values, not measurements. The achievable reduction and model quality are validated per workload.

Technical constraints

  • Unusual network events are rare and must survive distillation
  • Temporal dependencies (alarm sequences, load patterns) matter to the models
  • No interference with network operations systems

Potential economic outcomes

  • Less transport capacity, central ingestion and storage used for training data
  • Smaller central processing and training runs, net of regional distillation compute

Potential strategic outcomes

  • Network-wide anomaly models trained on data from every region
  • A pattern that scales as telemetry volumes grow

Technical validation requirements

  • Recall on rare anomalies and incident precursors
  • Preservation of the temporal patterns the task depends on
  • Compute footprint at the regional sites
  • Net reduction after distillation overhead

Relevant stakeholder CTO or head of network operations, with the head of data platforms

05Reference architecture

Reference architecture for pilot evaluation: it shows how the components would be placed, not an existing deployment.

  • Original (raw) data
  • Optimized (distilled) dataset
  • Model / results
  • Approved process (not a data path)
  • GroundTruth AI component
  • The runtime is placed on a gateway or site server with enough local compute and storage to buffer and process batches; constrained end devices are not targeted.
  • Raw data remains at the edge under the customer's existing local retention policy.

06Business outcomes

Potential outcomes; their size depends on the workload and is established in an evaluation.

Strategic business enablement

  • A scalable architecture for distributed AI that does not grow central costs linearly with device count.
  • Sites with limited connectivity become viable contributors to central models.
  • Potential reduction in energy spent transmitting data, subject to measurement in the customer environment.

Assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct cost reduction

  • Less uplink traffic attributable to AI training data.
  • Lower cloud ingestion and central storage for training datasets.
  • Lower central processing to filter raw data before training, net of edge-side distillation cost.

Operational efficiency

  • Less dependence on high-bandwidth connectivity at remote sites.
  • Training data selection driven by the model task rather than by link capacity.
  • A consistent data-preparation pattern across heterogeneous device fleets.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of eligible cost lines, net of distillation and evaluation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Technical considerations

For a pilot, the runtime is installed on one or a few customer-provided gateways or site servers and processes buffered batches on a fixed schedule. Packaging for fleet-wide rollout and remote updates is a proposed roadmap item.

Required infrastructure

  • A gateway or site server with a Python runtime and local buffer storage (sizing set during the pilot).
  • Access to locally buffered data for the pilot variables.
  • The existing uplink and central ingestion pipeline.

Operational constraints

  • Real-time or streaming distillation has not been validated; batch cadence is a design parameter.
  • Gateway compute, memory and power budgets limit dataset size per batch and must be measured.
  • Heterogeneous device types may require separate task definitions.

To be validated in an evaluation

Open questions

  • No benchmark yet on sensor time series, telemetry, logs or video from real device fleets.
  • No qualification of specific gateway or device hardware.
  • No measurement of end-to-end uplink and ingestion savings on customer infrastructure.
  • No production deployment.

Risks managed in the pilot

  • Gateway resources may be insufficient for the required batch size or cadence.
  • Rare events may be under-represented unless coverage constraints are enforced.

Data governance

  • No real-time execution or compatibility with specific edge devices is claimed; both must be validated on the customer's hardware during a pilot.

09Available evidence

Enterprise application for pilot evaluation — not yet validated in production

Internal experiments show large data reductions with limited accuracy change on public benchmarks, and an internal measurement indicates lower energy use on IoT / edge devices at the point of capture. Edge-fleet data, batch cadence and gateway overhead have not yet been measured on customer infrastructure.

What supports it today

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).
  • 39% less energy on IoT / edge devices at the point of capture (internal measurement; methodology not yet published; distillation's own energy not separately reported).

Enterprise pilot design

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One fleet-level model whose training data is currently uplinked in raw form from a set of sites or gateways.
Required inputs
  • Buffered raw data from a representative subset of sites or gateways
  • Event or failure labels where available
  • The current central training pipeline and evaluation metric
  • One or more gateways or site servers available for installation
Existing baseline
The existing model trained centrally on the raw uplinked data.
Evaluation methodology
  • Train the same model on raw and distilled data; evaluate both on the same held-out full-data test set.
  • Measure uplink volume, ingestion and central storage for both paths.
  • Measure gateway CPU, memory, energy and batch processing time.
Technical success criteria
  • Model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline.
  • Labeled events in the test window represented in distilled batches.
  • Distillation completes within the agreed batch window on target hardware.
Economic success criteria
  • Uplink and ingestion volume for training data reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Net saving remains positive after edge-side overhead.
Deliverables
  • Evaluation report (raw vs. distilled), including event coverage
  • Measured uplink, ingestion, storage, compute and energy comparison
  • Gateway sizing and placement recommendation
  • Fleet rollout plan and risk register
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • Target gateway hardware qualified
  • Security approval of packaging and update process
  • Named owner for task definitions and regeneration
