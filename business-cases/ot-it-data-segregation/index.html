BC01 Industrial data architecture · Flagship

OT/IT data segregation

Train enterprise AI on plant data without replicating the plant into IT.

Distill task-relevant training data inside the authorized OT environment and move only approved, optimized datasets across the OT/IT boundary for central model training.

Business problem

Central AI programs need plant data, but replicating full OT histories into IT erodes segregation and multiplies infrastructure.

Proposed solution

Task-aware distillation runs source-side, in the OT environment; only approved distilled datasets cross the boundary.

Primary outcome

Centralized industrial AI training without centralizing all raw OT data.

01Business challenge

Industrial enterprises keep Operational Technology (OT) and Information Technology (IT) environments apart for good reasons: safety, availability, cybersecurity, vendor support obligations and clear operational accountability. Plant historians, SCADA systems and control networks sit behind segmented zones, industrial DMZs and, in some sites, one-way gateways.

Enterprise AI programs pull in the opposite direction. Predictive maintenance, quality and energy models are usually trained centrally, on IT or cloud platforms, and the conventional workflow asks for large volumes of raw sensor history to be copied across the boundary first. Each new use case adds another replication path, another landing zone and another copy to secure, store and govern.

The result is a recurring trade-off between the architecture the plant needs and the data volume the central AI team asks for.

02Why existing approaches are insufficient

Replicate full OT history into the data lake

Maximizes data availability but multiplies boundary traffic, central storage and the number of raw copies under governance. Much of the replicated volume is never used by the models it was copied for.

Rule-based down-sampling or aggregation at the edge

Cheap and predictable, but task-agnostic: averaging and fixed sampling rates can remove exactly the transients and rare operating states a predictive model needs.

Train models locally at each site

Keeps data in place but fragments the model estate, duplicates ML skills and compute at every plant and makes cross-site learning difficult.

Federated learning

Avoids moving data but requires training infrastructure and orchestration inside each OT environment and a sustained, bidirectional model-exchange channel across the boundary, which many OT security policies do not allow.

Trade-offs describe generic architectural approaches, not specific products.

03GroundTruth AI solution

GroundTruth AI moves the data-reduction step to the source. A distillation runtime deployed in an authorized zone on the OT side builds a compact training dataset for a specific modeling task, validates it against the full data, and hands it to the existing, approved transfer path. Central teams train on the distilled dataset.

  1. Define the task

    The central AI team and plant engineering agree the target model, the variables and the acceptance thresholds (for example, maximum accuracy loss and minimum coverage of known failure modes).

  2. Distill at the source

    The runtime reads historian extracts inside the OT environment and generates a task-specific distilled dataset. Raw data never leaves its zone.

  3. Validate fidelity

    A fidelity report compares models trained on full and distilled data and checks coverage of operating regimes and labeled events.

  4. Approve and transfer

    OT and data owners approve the dataset; it crosses the boundary through the existing DMZ or gateway path, under existing controls.

  5. Train centrally

    Data science teams train and iterate on central platforms with a fraction of the original volume, and request regeneration when the task changes.

What this does not do

  • It does not replace network segmentation, firewalls, data diodes or OT security controls.
  • It does not open new connections into control networks; it uses transfer paths the customer already authorizes.
  • A distilled dataset is still plant data and stays under the customer's classification and transfer rules.

04Reference architecture

  • Raw data
  • Distilled dataset
  • Model / results
  • Control / approvals
  • GroundTruth AI component
  • The distillation runtime is deployed in a zone the OT owner authorizes (typically level 3 or the site operations zone), not on control-level devices.
  • Only the distilled dataset and its fidelity report cross the boundary; task specifications flow inward through the customer's existing change process.

05Business outcomes

Direct cost reduction

  • Less data replicated across the OT/IT boundary for each modeling use case.
  • Lower central storage and ingestion for training data.
  • Lower central compute per training run, net of source-side distillation cost.

Operational efficiency

  • Existing segregation architecture preserved: no new raw data paths into IT.
  • Fewer raw copies to secure, catalog and retire.
  • Faster onboarding of new plant use cases, because datasets are smaller and task-specific.

Strategic enablement

  • Central AI training on industrial data without centralizing all raw OT data.
  • A repeatable pattern for enterprise-wide industrial intelligence across plants.
  • Use cases previously blocked by data-transfer policy become candidates for evaluation.

Strategic value is assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of each cost line and are net of the cost of distillation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Implementation approach

For a pilot, the runtime is installed on a customer-provided server in the OT-side zone and operated offline from historian exports. Packaging as a hardened container or appliance for production is a proposed roadmap item, scoped with the customer's OT security team.

Required infrastructure

  • One server or VM in the authorized OT-side zone (CPU-first; sizing set during the pilot).
  • Read access to historian extracts for the pilot variables.
  • An approved outbound transfer path for files.

Operational constraints

  • Industrial time-series distillation has not yet been benchmarked; the pilot is the first measurement.
  • Labels for failure modes are often sparse; coverage targets must be agreed up front.
  • Change-control windows in OT can lengthen installation timelines.

09Evidence

Proposed enterprise application — not yet validated in production

Internal experiments show large data reductions with limited accuracy change on public benchmarks. Whether the same holds for industrial sensor time series, and for multi-variable failure prediction, is what an OT/IT pilot is designed to establish.

What supports it

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).
  • Distillation runs as a software step on a standard Python runtime, which is compatible with an offline, file-based OT deployment.

What is not yet validated

  • No benchmark yet on industrial time-series or historian data.
  • No measurement yet of rare-failure coverage after distillation.
  • No measured source-side compute and energy overhead on OT-zone hardware.
  • No production deployment or OT security assessment completed.

Risks we manage in the pilot

  • Distillation may under-represent rare operating states unless coverage constraints are enforced.
  • OT security approval of a new software component can delay or block deployment.

10Pilot proposal

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One predictive-maintenance or quality model that today requires a raw historian copy in IT.
Required inputs
  • 6–24 months of historian data for the selected assets, exported in the OT zone
  • Failure, maintenance or quality labels where available
  • The current central training pipeline and its evaluation metric
  • A server in the authorized OT-side zone
Existing baseline
The existing model trained centrally on the full replicated dataset.
Evaluation methodology
  • Train the same model on full and distilled data; evaluate both on the same held-out full-data test set.
  • Measure transfer volume, central storage and training compute for both paths.
  • Measure source-side distillation time, compute and energy.
  • Review placement and controls with OT security.
Technical success criteria
  • Model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline (agreed before the pilot).
  • All labeled failure modes in the test window represented in the distilled set.
  • No change to OT network paths or control-system load.
Economic success criteria
  • Boundary transfer and central training-data storage reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Net saving remains positive after source-side overhead.
Deliverables
  • Fidelity report (full vs. distilled), including coverage analysis
  • Measured transfer, storage, compute and energy comparison
  • Reference architecture for the customer's zone model
  • Production adoption plan and risk register
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • OT security approval of runtime placement and packaging
  • Data owner sign-off on the approval workflow
  • Named owner for regeneration when the task or process changes
