Discuss your OT/IT architecture

01Business challenge

Large industrial organizations keep Operational Technology (OT) and Information Technology (IT) environments apart on purpose: for operational independence, industrial cybersecurity, data governance, infrastructure architecture, production continuity, organizational ownership and regulatory requirements. Plant historians, SCADA systems and control networks sit behind segmented zones, industrial DMZs and, at some sites, one-way gateways.

Enterprise AI programs pull in the opposite direction. Predictive maintenance, quality and energy models are usually trained centrally, and the conventional workflow first asks for large volumes of historical industrial data to be copied into enterprise IT. Each new use case adds another replication path, another landing zone and another raw copy to secure and govern.

The problem is not only that data transfer is expensive. Complete raw data centralization is often undesirable, operationally difficult or simply not allowed, so valuable cross-plant analytics never start. The question is how to give central AI teams what their models need without moving the plant's full history.

02Business opportunity

Centralized industrial AI training without centralizing all raw OT data.

Where conventional architectures fall short

Replicate full OT history into the data lake

Maximizes data availability but multiplies boundary traffic, central storage and the number of raw copies under governance. Much of the replicated volume is never used by the models it was copied for.

Rule-based down-sampling or aggregation at the edge

Cheap and predictable, but task-agnostic: averaging and fixed sampling rates can remove exactly the transients and rare operating states a predictive model needs.

Train models locally at each site

Keeps data in place but fragments the model estate, duplicates ML skills and compute at every plant and makes cross-site learning difficult.

Federated learning

Avoids moving data but requires training infrastructure and orchestration inside each OT environment and a sustained, bidirectional model-exchange channel across the boundary, which many OT security policies do not allow.

Generic architectural approaches, not specific products. How distillation compares with other techniques.

03GroundTruth AI approach

GroundTruth AI moves the data-reduction step to the source. A distillation runtime deployed in an authorized zone on the OT side builds a compact training dataset for a specific modeling task, validates it against the full data, and hands it to the existing, approved transfer path. Central teams train on the distilled dataset.

  1. Define the task

    The central AI team and plant engineering agree the target model, the variables and the acceptance thresholds (for example, maximum accuracy loss and minimum coverage of known failure modes).

  2. Distill close to the source

    The engine reads authorized historian extracts in an approved OT-side computing environment and generates a compact, task-specific dataset. Original data stays in its zone, under existing retention and operating policies.

  3. Evaluate downstream performance

    The same model is trained on full and on distilled data and both are compared on the same test data. Coverage of labeled events and operating regimes is checked against the targets agreed for the evaluation. Training the full-data reference model is part of the evaluation cost.

  4. Approve and transfer

    OT and data owners approve the dataset; it crosses the boundary through the existing DMZ or gateway path, under existing controls.

  5. Train centrally

    Data science teams train and iterate on enterprise platforms with the approved datasets, and request a new distilled dataset through the change process when the task changes.

What this does not do

  • It does not replace network segmentation, firewalls, data diodes or OT security controls.
  • It does not open new connections into control networks, connect control systems to the cloud or bypass existing security controls; it uses transfer paths the customer already authorizes.
  • It does not run on control-level devices or change safety-critical control loops.
  • A distilled dataset is still plant data and stays under the customer's classification and transfer rules.

04Illustrative enterprise scenario

Illustrative enterprise scenario Hypothetical organization; assumptions are for discussion and not contractual.

Global manufacturer, 20 facilities

Enterprise context
A manufacturing group operates 20 industrial facilities. Each facility keeps its operational data in an authorized OT environment, behind its own industrial DMZ.
Current architecture
Plant historians record sensor and equipment data locally. Central analytics receive selected KPIs, but the enterprise AI team has no governed path for training data at scale.
Business problem
The central AI team wants to train predictive-maintenance models on data from all 20 facilities. The conventional approach would replicate about 200 TB of eligible history into enterprise IT, a request that OT security, plant owners and infrastructure budgets all resist.
Proposed GroundTruth AI architecture
A distillation engine runs in each facility's authorized site-operations zone. It builds a task-specific dataset for the predictive-maintenance model, validates it, and, after owner approval, passes it through the existing DMZ transfer path. The central team trains one model on the combined approved datasets.

Illustrative assumptions

  • 20 facilities × 10 TB of eligible training data = 200 TB
  • Assumed dataset reduction: 90%, an illustrative planning value, not a measured result for this workload
  • Optimized data transferred: 20 TB
  • Potential avoided transfer: 180 TB

Planning values, not measurements. The achievable reduction and model quality are validated per workload.

Technical constraints

  • Raw historian data stays in each facility, under existing retention policies
  • Only existing, approved OT/IT exchange mechanisms are used
  • No change to control systems or safety-critical control loops

Potential economic outcomes

  • About 180 TB of raw training copies not transferred, ingested or stored centrally (illustrative)
  • Smaller central training runs, net of source-side distillation compute

Potential strategic outcomes

  • Enterprise-wide predictive maintenance across all facilities
  • Analytical collaboration between plants without a central raw OT data lake
  • A repeatable pattern for the next industrial use case

Technical validation requirements

  • Predictive performance within the agreed tolerance of a full-data baseline
  • Coverage of rare failure modes in every facility's distilled dataset
  • Cross-site generalization, including a facility held out from training
  • Source-side compute and energy overhead measured on site hardware

05Reference architecture

Reference architecture for pilot evaluation: it shows how the components would be placed, not an existing deployment.

  • Original (raw) data
  • Optimized (distilled) dataset
  • Model / results
  • Approved process (not a data path)
  • GroundTruth AI component
  • Raw data retained at source: historians and control systems keep their data under existing retention and operating policies. Nothing is deleted or moved by default.
  • The engine runs in a computing environment the OT owner approves (typically the level 3 site operations zone), never on control-level devices, and it does not alter control loops.
  • Only approved distilled datasets, with their evaluation results, cross the boundary, through the existing DMZ or gateway. There is no direct cloud connectivity from industrial control systems and no bypass of existing security controls.
  • The dotted return path is an administrative change process (an approved task specification installed by OT staff), not a network connection. The data path is one-way, so the pattern also fits sites that use unidirectional gateways or data diodes.

06Business outcomes

Potential outcomes; their size depends on the workload and is established in an evaluation.

Strategic business enablement

  • Centralized AI development on OT data without complete raw OT data consolidation.
  • Original industrial data retained in authorized OT environments.
  • A repeatable, boundary-respecting pattern for each new industrial use case.
  • Use cases previously blocked by OT/IT transfer policy become candidates for evaluation.
  • A foundation for multi-facility models (see Distributed industrial intelligence).

Assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct cost reduction

  • Less raw industrial data replicated across the OT/IT boundary for each modeling use case.
  • Lower central storage and ingestion for training data.
  • Lower central compute per training run, net of source-side distillation cost.

Operational efficiency

  • Existing segregation architecture preserved: no new raw data paths into IT.
  • Fewer raw copies to secure, catalog and retire.
  • Faster onboarding of new plant use cases, because datasets are smaller and task-specific.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of eligible cost lines, net of distillation and evaluation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Technical considerations

For a pilot, the runtime is installed on a customer-provided server in the OT-side zone and operated offline from historian exports. Packaging as a hardened container or appliance for production is a proposed roadmap item, scoped with the customer's OT security team.

Required infrastructure

  • One server or VM in the authorized OT-side zone (CPU-first; sizing set during the pilot).
  • Read access to historian extracts for the pilot variables.
  • An approved outbound transfer path for files.

Operational constraints

  • Industrial time-series distillation has not yet been benchmarked; the pilot is the first measurement.
  • Labels for failure modes are often sparse; coverage targets must be agreed up front.
  • Change-control windows in OT can lengthen installation timelines.

To be validated in an evaluation

Open questions

  • No benchmark yet on industrial time-series or historian data.
  • No measurement yet of rare-failure coverage after distillation.
  • No measured source-side compute and energy overhead on OT-zone hardware.
  • No production deployment or OT security assessment completed.

Risks managed in the pilot

  • Distillation may under-represent rare operating states unless coverage constraints are enforced.
  • OT security approval of a new software component can delay or block deployment.

Data governance

  • Distilled datasets derived from OT data remain subject to the customer's data classification, export-control and transfer policies.

09Available evidence

Enterprise application for pilot evaluation — not yet validated in production

Internal experiments show large data reductions with limited accuracy change on public benchmarks. Whether the same holds for industrial sensor time series, and for multi-variable failure prediction, is what an OT/IT pilot is designed to establish.

What supports it today

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).
  • Distillation runs as a software step on a standard Python runtime, which is compatible with an offline, file-based OT deployment.

Enterprise pilot design

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One predictive-maintenance or quality model that today requires a raw historian copy in IT.
Required inputs
  • 6–24 months of historian data for the selected assets, exported in the OT zone
  • Failure, maintenance or quality labels where available
  • The current central training pipeline and its evaluation metric
  • A server in the authorized OT-side zone
Existing baseline
The existing model trained centrally on the full replicated dataset.
Evaluation methodology
  • Train the same model on full and distilled data; evaluate both on the same held-out full-data test set.
  • Measure transfer volume, central storage and training compute for both paths.
  • Measure source-side distillation time, compute and energy.
  • Review placement and controls with OT security.
Technical success criteria
  • Model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline (agreed before the pilot).
  • All labeled failure modes in the test window represented in the distilled set.
  • No change to OT network paths or control-system load.
Economic success criteria
  • Boundary transfer and central training-data storage reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Net saving remains positive after source-side overhead.
Deliverables
  • Evaluation report (full vs. distilled), including coverage analysis
  • Measured transfer, storage, compute and energy comparison
  • Reference architecture for the customer's zone model
  • Production adoption plan and risk register
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • OT security approval of runtime placement and packaging
  • Data owner sign-off on the approval workflow
  • Named owner for regeneration when the task or process changes
