BC02 Distributed data estates · Flagship

Cross-enterprise analytics

Build group-wide models without first consolidating every subsidiary's data.

Generate task-specific distilled datasets inside each authorized data domain and combine the approved datasets for central analytics and AI training, while data ownership stays distributed.

Business problem

Group-wide analytics stall because subsidiaries, regions and business units hold data in separate, separately governed estates.

Proposed solution

Each domain distills a task-specific dataset in place; only approved distilled sets are combined centrally for training.

Primary outcome

Enterprise-wide predictive models without a complete data consolidation program.

01Business challenge

Large organizations rarely have one data estate. Subsidiaries, business units, regions and acquired companies each run their own platforms, with their own data owners, retention rules, security models and, often, their own regulators. That separation is usually deliberate: it reflects legal entities, accountability and risk boundaries.

Yet the most valuable analytical questions are group-wide: customer behavior across brands, risk across portfolios, demand across regions, outcomes across clinical sites. The default answer is a consolidation program: replicate everything into a central lake or warehouse, harmonize it, then start modeling. Those programs are expensive, slow to approve and frequently constrained by governance policies that do not permit full replication in the first place.

Analytical ambition ends up waiting for data architecture, and many cross-business use cases never reach the evaluation stage.

02Why existing approaches are insufficient

Full consolidation into a central lake or warehouse

Delivers one place to model, but requires replicating complete datasets, multi-year integration effort and approvals from every data owner. Policy may simply not allow it.

Data virtualization or federated query

Avoids copies for reporting, but ML training needs repeated full scans of large datasets across domains, which shifts cost and latency onto source systems.

Separate models per subsidiary

Respects ownership but produces many small models, duplicated effort and no group-wide view of patterns that only appear across entities.

Federated learning

Keeps data in place but needs training infrastructure, orchestration and ML operations inside every domain, plus a coordinated model-exchange process that many organizations are not yet equipped to run.

Trade-offs describe generic architectural approaches, not specific products.

03GroundTruth AI solution

GroundTruth AI changes what has to move. Within each authorized data domain, a distillation runtime builds a compact dataset for one defined analytical task and validates it against that domain's full data. Data owners approve what leaves; the central team combines the approved distilled datasets and trains group-wide models.

  1. Agree the shared task

    Group analytics and domain data owners define the target model, a common feature contract and acceptance thresholds before any data moves.

  2. Distill in each domain

    The runtime runs inside each subsidiary's or region's environment and produces a task-specific distilled dataset. Source data stays where it is owned.

  3. Validate locally

    Each domain receives a fidelity report: a model trained on its distilled set compared with one trained on its full data.

  4. Approve and combine

    Domain owners approve release under their own policies; approved datasets are combined in the central environment.

  5. Train and evaluate centrally

    The central team trains the group-wide model and evaluates it on held-out data from each domain, so no domain is silently under-served.

What this does not do

  • It does not harmonize schemas or master data: a shared feature contract is a prerequisite.
  • It does not anonymize data or authorize any transfer; distilled sets keep the source data's obligations.
  • It does not replace a data integration strategy where full integration is the right answer.

04Reference architecture

  • Raw data
  • Distilled dataset
  • Model / results
  • Control / approvals
  • GroundTruth AI component
  • Each runtime operates under the governance of the domain that hosts it; the central team never receives credentials to source systems.
  • Combining distilled sets from heterogeneous domains is an architectural hypothesis not yet benchmarked: differences in populations and distributions between domains are measured explicitly in the pilot.

05Business outcomes

Direct cost reduction

  • Less data replicated into central platforms for each group-wide use case.
  • Lower central storage, ingestion and training compute for those use cases, net of in-domain distillation cost.
  • Smaller transfers between domains and the central environment.

Operational efficiency

  • Analytical projects start before, or without, a full consolidation program.
  • Fewer raw copies of subsidiary data to secure, catalog and retire.
  • One repeatable release pattern that each data owner can approve under existing policy.

Strategic enablement

  • Group-wide predictive models that reflect patterns across entities, not one entity at a time.
  • Distributed data ownership preserved as a design principle rather than an obstacle.
  • Analytical collaboration across organizational boundaries that was previously not attempted.

Strategic value is assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of each cost line and are net of the cost of distillation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Implementation approach

A pilot typically involves two or three domains. The runtime is installed in each domain's environment and run against agreed extracts; distilled datasets are released through the customer's existing approval and transfer process. Automated, scheduled multi-domain orchestration is a proposed roadmap item.

Required infrastructure

  • One VM or container host inside each participating domain (CPU-first; sizing set in the pilot).
  • Read access to an extract of the agreed features in each domain.
  • A central environment authorized to receive the approved distilled datasets.

Operational constraints

  • A common feature contract across domains is required; semantic differences are not resolved by distillation.
  • Distribution differences between domains may require per-domain coverage targets.
  • Legal review of each transfer remains necessary and can set the pilot's critical path.

09Evidence

Proposed enterprise application — not yet validated in production

The single-dataset results are encouraging: large reductions with limited accuracy change on public benchmarks. The cross-enterprise pattern adds an untested step, combining distilled sets produced independently in heterogeneous domains, which is an architectural hypothesis the pilot is designed to test.

What supports it

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).
  • Distillation runs as a self-contained software step, so it can be executed separately inside each domain.

What is not yet validated

  • Combining distilled sets from heterogeneous domains has not been benchmarked (non-IID data).
  • No measurement yet of per-domain performance gaps in a combined model.
  • No enterprise-scale or multi-domain deployment completed.
  • No legal or privacy assessment of distilled datasets in a real governance setting.

Risks we manage in the pilot

  • A domain with a distinct population may be under-represented in the group model unless its performance is tracked separately.
  • Approval processes across several data owners can extend timelines beyond the technical work.

10Pilot proposal

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One group-wide predictive or analytical model that today requires consolidating data from two or three domains.
Required inputs
  • An extract of the agreed features in each participating domain
  • A shared target definition and feature contract
  • Where permitted, a consolidated full-data baseline or a per-domain baseline
  • A host in each domain and a central environment for the combined set
Existing baseline
The same model trained on consolidated full data where allowed; otherwise per-domain models trained on each domain's full data.
Evaluation methodology
  • Distill independently in each domain against the shared task.
  • Train the group model on combined distilled sets; evaluate overall and per domain on held-out full data.
  • Measure replicated volume, central storage and training compute against the baseline path.
  • Record in-domain overhead and the approval cycle time for each release.
Technical success criteria
  • Group model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline.
  • No participating domain below its agreed per-domain threshold.
  • Distilled releases approved by each data owner under existing policy.
Economic success criteria
  • Replicated volume and central storage reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Net saving positive after in-domain distillation overhead.
Deliverables
  • Per-domain fidelity reports and combined-model evaluation
  • Measured comparison of replication, storage and compute
  • Reference architecture and release workflow for the customer's domains
  • Production adoption plan, including legal and governance checkpoints
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • Legal and data-protection sign-off on the release pattern
  • Data-owner agreement to a recurring approval workflow
  • Feature contract owned and maintained by a named team
