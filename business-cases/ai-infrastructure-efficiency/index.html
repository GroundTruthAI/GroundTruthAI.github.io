01Business challenge

AI workloads are among the fastest-growing consumers of enterprise compute, storage and power. GPU clusters, high-performance storage tiers and the cooling that supports them are expensive to expand, and in many facilities power and rack capacity, not budget, set the ceiling.

A large share of this capacity is consumed by training and retraining on datasets that contain substantial redundancy. Every redundant record is stored on fast tiers, read on every epoch and processed on every training run.

Infrastructure leaders are asked to support more models with the same footprint, while finance and sustainability teams ask for clearer economics per workload.

02Business opportunity

Better utilization of AI infrastructure for eligible workloads, net of distillation overhead.

Where conventional architectures fall short

Add capacity

Buys headroom but increases capital, power and cooling commitments, and is constrained where facility power or hardware supply is the limit.

Scheduling and utilization tuning

Improves how capacity is shared but does not reduce the work each training job performs.

Storage tiering and lifecycle policies

Lowers the cost of cold data but does not reduce the data that active training jobs read repeatedly.

Model compression and efficient architectures

Reduces model size and inference cost; training data volume, and the compute it drives, often remains unchanged. These techniques are complementary to dataset reduction.

Generic architectural approaches, not specific products. How distillation compares with other techniques.

03GroundTruth AI approach

GroundTruth AI reduces the data side of eligible AI workloads. For training and retraining jobs where dataset volume drives compute and storage, the distillation engine produces a validated, task-specific dataset that replaces the full dataset in the training pipeline. The saving is assessed per workload, after subtracting the compute, energy and operational cost of running distillation.

  1. Identify eligible workloads

    Select recurring training jobs where data volume is a material driver of compute, storage or energy.

  2. Distill per task

    Generate a distilled dataset for each selected workload, with customer-defined accuracy tolerances.

  3. Validate

    Compare models trained on full and distilled data on the same held-out test set.

  4. Substitute in the pipeline

    Point the training job at the distilled dataset; keep the full dataset under existing retention policy.

  5. Measure net effect

    Track storage, GPU/CPU hours and energy per workload, including distillation overhead.

What this does not do

  • Dataset reduction does not translate into an equivalent reduction in total data center spend; only the data-proportional share of eligible workloads is affected.
  • It does not reduce inference, networking or facility overhead costs that are independent of training data volume.
  • It does not replace capacity planning, scheduling or hardware efficiency programs.

Enterprise private AI platform

Enterprise context
An enterprise runs a private AI platform in its own data centers, shared by dozens of teams.
Current architecture
Training datasets are stored with backups and replicas, and training jobs compete for the same capacity.
Business problem
Storage, compute and energy demand grow with data volume, and capacity requests outpace budgets.
Proposed GroundTruth AI architecture
Eligible workloads train on validated distilled datasets, and the capacity they no longer use is reallocated. Source data is retained under existing policies.

  • Distillation itself consumes compute and energy

Potential economic outcomes

  • Less data-proportional compute for eligible workloads

Potential strategic outcomes

  • Capacity expansion deferred for eligible workloads

Technical validation requirements

  • Utilization measured before and after on the same workloads
  • Energy measured including distillation
  • Model quality within tolerance

06Business outcomes

Potential outcomes; their size depends on the workload and is established in an evaluation.

Strategic business enablement

  • Capacity headroom where power or hardware supply constrains growth.
  • An input to AI infrastructure planning that addresses data volume, not only hardware.
  • Measured efficiency data that can support sustainability reporting once lifecycle measurement is in place.

Assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct cost reduction

  • Lower GPU/CPU hours per eligible training run, net of distillation compute.
  • Less high-performance storage for active training datasets.
  • Potentially lower energy per eligible training run, measured including distillation.

Operational efficiency

  • More training jobs supported on existing capacity.
  • Shorter training queues and faster turnaround for data science teams.
  • Clearer per-workload infrastructure economics.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of eligible cost lines, net of distillation and evaluation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Technical considerations

For a pilot, distillation runs as a batch job inside the customer's data platform on one or two selected training workloads. Integration as a standard pipeline step is a proposed roadmap item.

Required infrastructure

  • Compute capacity for distillation jobs on the existing cluster or a dedicated node.
  • Access to training datasets for the selected workloads.
  • Per-job metering of compute and, where available, energy.

Operational constraints

  • Only workloads where data volume drives cost benefit; fixed overheads are unaffected.
  • Energy measurement requires per-job metering, which many clusters lack.
  • Distillation has been benchmarked on small public datasets; scaling to enterprise volumes is untested.

To be validated in an evaluation

Open questions

  • No measurement on GPU clusters or enterprise-scale datasets.
  • No end-to-end accounting of distillation compute and energy against training savings.
  • No data center or lifecycle energy measurement.
  • No production deployment.

Risks managed in the pilot

  • Savings may be small for workloads where data volume is not the main cost driver.
  • Distillation overhead may offset savings for infrequently trained models.

Data governance

  • Dataset reduction is not equivalent to a reduction in total data center expenditure. Only the data-proportional share of eligible workloads is affected, net of the compute and energy used by distillation.

09Available evidence

Enterprise application for pilot evaluation — not yet validated in production

Internal experiments show large data reductions with limited accuracy change on public benchmarks, and an internal measurement indicates lower energy per training run on a workstation. Effects at data center scale, and net of distillation overhead, have not yet been measured.

What supports it today

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).
  • 86% less energy per training run on a workstation after data reduction (internal measurement; distillation's own energy not separately reported).

Enterprise pilot design

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One or two recurring training workloads with material compute, storage or energy consumption.
Required inputs
  • Training datasets and pipelines for the selected workloads
  • Current compute, storage and, where available, energy metering per job
  • The evaluation metric and acceptance tolerance for each model
  • Cluster capacity to run distillation jobs
Existing baseline
The current training jobs on full datasets, metered over representative runs.
Evaluation methodology
  • Train each model on full and distilled data; evaluate both on the same held-out test set.
  • Meter compute, storage and energy for training runs and for distillation separately.
  • Calculate net effect per run and per year at the customer's retraining frequency.
Technical success criteria
  • Model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline.
  • Distilled datasets integrate with existing pipelines without code changes beyond the data path.
Economic success criteria
  • Net reduction in compute hours and energy per run, including distillation, above a customer-defined target.
  • Positive annualized net saving for the selected workloads.
Deliverables
  • Evaluation report per workload
  • Metered compute, storage and energy comparison, including distillation overhead
  • Workload eligibility assessment for the wider training portfolio
  • Production adoption plan
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • Agreed method for metering and reporting net effect
  • ML platform owner approval of the pipeline integration
