01Business challenge

Industrial organizations run many facilities: plants, substations, mines, depots, pumping stations. Each has its own data collection systems, equipment generations, control configurations and operating conditions, and each holds years of sensor history.

The most valuable failure knowledge is spread thin. A given failure mode may occur a handful of times per site per year, so no single facility has enough examples to train a reliable model. Learning across sites means pooling data, but shipping complete historian archives from every facility to a central platform is often impractical: limited site connectivity, high transfer and storage cost, local data ownership and OT security policies all stand in the way.

Organizations end up with either isolated site models of uneven quality or a stalled centralization project.

02Business opportunity

Shared predictive models that learn across facilities and operating conditions.

Where conventional architectures fall short

Centralize all historian data

Gives data scientists everything, but moves and stores complete archives from every site, most of which is steady-state operation of little value to the model.

Independent models per facility

Respects local ownership but each model sees only its own rare events, so performance is weakest exactly where failures are scarcest.

Fixed down-sampling or aggregation at each site

Reduces volume predictably, but averages away transients and rare regimes that drive failure prediction.

Federated learning across sites

Avoids moving data but needs training infrastructure at every site and continuous model exchange, which strains site IT capacity and OT change control.

Generic architectural approaches, not specific products. How distillation compares with other techniques.

03GroundTruth AI approach

GroundTruth AI distributes data preparation and centralizes learning. A distillation runtime at each facility builds a task-specific distilled dataset from local history, with explicit targets for rare-event and operating-regime coverage. Approved datasets from all sites are combined to train shared models centrally.

  1. Define the shared model

    Reliability engineering and the central AI team agree the target (for example, a failure mode on a common asset class), the feature contract and coverage targets.

  2. Distill at each facility

    The runtime processes local history on site and keeps every labeled failure event and each operating regime represented, while compressing routine operation.

  3. Validate per site

    Each site's distilled set is evaluated against its own full data, and rare-event and regime coverage is assessed against the agreed targets.

  4. Approve and combine

    Site and OT owners approve release through existing transfer paths; approved sets are combined centrally.

  5. Train and evaluate across sites

    Shared models are trained centrally and evaluated on held-out data from each site, so differences between facilities are visible, not averaged away.

What this does not do

  • It does not harmonize tag names, units or asset hierarchies across sites; that mapping is a prerequisite.
  • It does not create failure labels; where labels are sparse, coverage targets must reflect that.
  • It does not bypass site OT security controls or connectivity limits; it reduces what has to travel.

04Illustrative enterprise scenario

Illustrative enterprise scenario Hypothetical organization; assumptions are for discussion and not contractual.

Multinational energy company

Enterprise context
An energy company operates generation and distribution assets in several countries: plants, substations and wind farms with different equipment generations and operating regimes.
Current architecture
Operational data stays in regional control environments and plant historians. Central analytics see aggregated reports; asset-health models are built region by region, with uneven quality.
Business problem
A central analytics team wants global asset-health, failure-prediction and operational-forecasting models. Rare failures are spread thinly across regions, so no single region has enough examples, yet consolidating every historian across segregated infrastructure is impractical.
Proposed GroundTruth AI architecture
Each region distills task-specific datasets locally, with rare-failure and operating-regime coverage set as evaluation targets. Approved datasets move through authorized exchange paths to the central team, which trains a shared model and evaluates it region by region.

Technical constraints

  • Infrastructure segregation between regions and between OT and IT
  • Different operating regimes across sites, seasons and asset classes
  • Time-series dependencies the forecasting models rely on
  • Rare failures that must survive distillation
  • Authorized data exchange only, through existing mechanisms

Potential economic outcomes

  • Less raw time-series history replicated to the center for each model
  • Lower central storage and training compute for fleet-wide models

Potential strategic outcomes

  • Global asset-health and failure-prediction models that learn from rare failures in every region
  • Comparable models and metrics across the asset fleet
  • Regional data ownership preserved

Technical validation requirements

  • Time-series structure (sequences, lags, seasonality) preserved for the task
  • Rare-failure coverage per region and asset class
  • Performance per operating regime, not only on average
  • Performance on a region held out from training

Relevant stakeholder Head of asset management or reliability, with the Chief Data Officer

05Reference architecture

Reference architecture for pilot evaluation: it shows how the components would be placed, not an existing deployment.

  • Original (raw) data
  • Optimized (distilled) dataset
  • Model / results
  • Approved process (not a data path)
  • GroundTruth AI component
  • Coverage targets are set per site: every labeled failure event and each identified operating regime is retained, so rare events are concentrated rather than diluted.
  • Combining distilled sets from heterogeneous sites is an architectural hypothesis not yet benchmarked: distribution differences between facilities are measured explicitly in the pilot.

06Business outcomes

Potential outcomes; their size depends on the workload and is established in an evaluation.

Strategic business enablement

  • Multi-site predictive maintenance and shared equipment health models.
  • Cross-facility anomaly detection that learns from failures wherever they occur.
  • Cross-site analytical collaboration and models that generalize across operating conditions.

Assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct cost reduction

  • Less data transferred from facilities to the central platform per use case.
  • Lower central storage and training compute for cross-site models, net of on-site distillation cost.
  • Lower dependence on high-bandwidth links from remote facilities.

Operational efficiency

  • Data preparation runs where the data and the process knowledge are.
  • New facilities join a shared model by adding a runtime, not a full archive migration.
  • Fewer raw archive copies to secure and govern centrally.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of eligible cost lines, net of distillation and evaluation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Technical considerations

A pilot typically covers two or three facilities with a common asset class. The runtime runs offline against site exports; distilled datasets travel through existing transfer paths. Fleet-wide orchestration and remote management of site runtimes are proposed roadmap items.

Required infrastructure

  • One server or VM per participating facility in an authorized zone (CPU-first; sizing set in the pilot).
  • Read access to historian extracts and maintenance or failure labels at each site.
  • A central environment authorized to receive approved datasets.

Operational constraints

  • Tag, unit and asset-hierarchy mapping across sites must exist or be built for the pilot assets.
  • Failure labels are sparse and inconsistent between sites; label quality bounds model quality.
  • Industrial time-series distillation has not yet been benchmarked; the pilot is the first measurement.

To be validated in an evaluation

Open questions

  • No benchmark on industrial time-series or historian data.
  • No study yet of rare-failure coverage after distillation.
  • No experiment combining distilled sets from sites with different distributions (non-IID).
  • No multi-site or production deployment completed.

Risks managed in the pilot

  • A facility with unusual equipment or conditions may be poorly served by a shared model; per-site evaluation is mandatory.
  • Rare failure modes may be lost if coverage constraints are not enforced.
  • Site-by-site OT approvals can extend timelines.

Data governance

  • Shared models must be evaluated per facility; an improvement on average can hide a degradation at one site.

09Available evidence

Enterprise application for pilot evaluation — not yet validated in production

Single-dataset benchmarks show large reductions with limited accuracy change. This business case adds three untested elements: industrial time-series data, explicit rare-event and regime coverage, and the combination of distilled sets from heterogeneous sites, which is an architectural hypothesis not yet benchmarked.

What supports it today

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).
  • Distillation runs as a self-contained software step that can be executed independently at each site.

Enterprise pilot design

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One failure mode or anomaly-detection task on an asset class present at two or three facilities.
Required inputs
  • 12–36 months of historian data for the selected assets at each facility
  • Maintenance, failure or event labels per site
  • A mapping of tags and units across sites for the pilot assets
  • A server per facility in an authorized zone
Existing baseline
A cross-site model trained on pooled full data where feasible, and each site's own model trained on its full data.
Evaluation methodology
  • Distill at each site with agreed rare-event and regime coverage targets.
  • Train the shared model on combined distilled sets; evaluate overall and per site on held-out full data.
  • Compare against site-only models and, where available, a pooled full-data model.
  • Measure transfer volume, central storage, training compute and on-site overhead.
Technical success criteria
  • Shared-model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline at every site.
  • All labeled failure events in the evaluation window retained in distilled sets.
  • No change to OT network paths or control-system load at any site.
Economic success criteria
  • Inter-site transfer and central storage reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Net saving positive after on-site distillation overhead.
Deliverables
  • Per-site fidelity and coverage reports
  • Shared-model evaluation, overall and per facility
  • Measured transfer, storage, compute and energy comparison
  • Fleet roll-out plan and risk register
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met at every pilot site
  • OT security approval of the site runtime pattern
  • Tag-mapping and label ownership assigned
  • Regeneration policy for process and equipment changes
