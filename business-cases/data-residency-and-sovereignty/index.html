BC07 Distributed data governance

Data residency and sovereignty

Keep original data where it must live, and still collaborate on analytics.

Generate approved, task-specific datasets inside authorized source environments, potentially reducing the need to transfer complete original datasets across jurisdictions or organizations.

Business problem

Multinational and regulated organizations must keep original data in specific jurisdictions or domains, which limits shared analytics.

Proposed solution

Distillation runs inside each authorized environment; only approved task-specific datasets are considered for permitted transfer.

Primary outcome

Less cross-border replication of complete datasets, with source data ownership preserved.

01Business challenge

Multinational and regulated organizations often need to retain original data within specific countries, regions or legal entities. Data protection law, sector regulation, contractual commitments and customer expectations all shape where data may be stored and processed.

Group-level analytics and AI still need data from every region. The conventional route is to copy complete datasets to a central platform, which may require legal assessments, transfer mechanisms and additional safeguards for each flow, and in some cases is not permitted at all.

Many organizations therefore run fragmented regional models or postpone group-wide initiatives while the data question is resolved.

02Why existing approaches are insufficient

Centralize complete datasets under transfer mechanisms

Enables group analytics where permitted, but every complete copy expands the scope of transfer assessments, safeguards and ongoing compliance obligations.

Regional platforms with separate models

Respects residency but duplicates infrastructure and skills, and limits learning across regions.

Aggregated statistics only

Lower sensitivity, but aggregates often lack the granularity needed to train predictive models.

Federated learning or privacy-enhancing technologies

Can keep data local, but require training infrastructure in every jurisdiction, specialized orchestration and their own legal assessment; maturity and cost vary widely by use case.

Trade-offs describe generic architectural approaches, not specific products.

03GroundTruth AI solution

GroundTruth AI runs distillation inside each authorized source environment. For a defined analytical task, it produces a compact training dataset and a fidelity report. The data owner and the customer's legal and privacy functions then decide whether, and under which safeguards, that dataset may be shared. Where transfer is permitted, the volume moved is smaller and task-specific; original datasets stay where they are.

  1. Define the task and legal basis

    The central team, data owners and legal/privacy functions agree the analytical task and the conditions under which derived datasets may be shared.

  2. Distill in each jurisdiction

    The runtime operates inside each authorized regional environment; original data does not leave it.

  3. Validate and review

    Each distilled dataset is accompanied by a fidelity report and reviewed by the data owner and privacy function before any transfer.

  4. Transfer only where permitted

    Approved datasets move through existing, authorized transfer mechanisms.

  5. Analyze centrally

    Group teams train and evaluate models on the combined approved datasets.

What this does not do

  • Distillation is not anonymization: distilled datasets may still contain or reveal personal or confidential information.
  • Distilled datasets remain subject to applicable data protection, residency, transfer and contractual restrictions.
  • Distillation does not provide legal authorization for any cross-border transfer; that decision remains with the customer's legal and privacy functions.

04Reference architecture

  • Raw data
  • Distilled dataset
  • Model / results
  • Control / approvals
  • GroundTruth AI component
  • Each runtime operates inside the authorized environment of its jurisdiction or legal entity.
  • The review step is a customer governance control; GroundTruth AI supplies the fidelity report, not the legal assessment.

05Business outcomes

Direct cost reduction

  • Smaller volumes moved through cross-border transfer mechanisms, where transfer is permitted.
  • Less central storage of complete regional dataset copies.
  • Lower central processing for group training data, net of regional distillation cost.

Operational efficiency

  • Fewer complete copies of regional data to secure, catalog and retire.
  • Task-specific datasets that are easier to describe in transfer and privacy assessments.
  • A repeatable pattern for onboarding additional regions.

Strategic enablement

  • Group-level analytics without complete centralization of original data.
  • Source data ownership preserved with regional entities.
  • Support for distributed data architectures aligned with residency objectives.

Strategic value is assessed qualitatively; no monetary value is assigned.

Direct savings apply only to the data-proportional share of each cost line and are net of the cost of distillation. Model them with your own assumptions.

08Implementation approach

For a pilot, runtimes are installed in two regional environments and operate on exports. Combination and evaluation happen centrally only if the customer's legal and privacy functions approve the transfer of the distilled datasets; otherwise evaluation stays local.

Required infrastructure

  • A compute environment inside each participating jurisdiction or legal entity.
  • Read access to regional data for the pilot variables.
  • A documented review and approval workflow owned by the customer.

Operational constraints

  • Combining distilled datasets from heterogeneous regions is an architectural hypothesis not yet benchmarked.
  • Legal review timelines are customer-controlled and may dominate pilot duration.
  • Some data categories may not be transferable in any form; those remain out of scope.

09Evidence

Proposed enterprise application — not yet validated in production

Internal experiments show large data reductions with limited accuracy change on single public benchmarks. Distilling separately in several jurisdictions and combining the results is an architectural hypothesis that has not yet been tested, and no privacy or re-identification assessment of distilled datasets has been performed.

What supports it

  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp (experimentally supported technical outcome).
  • Distillation runs on a standard Python runtime that can be deployed inside separate regional environments.

What is not yet validated

  • No experiment yet on combining distilled datasets from heterogeneous (non-IID) sources.
  • No privacy, disclosure-risk or re-identification assessment of distilled datasets.
  • No legal analysis of distilled datasets under any specific regulation.
  • No production deployment.

Risks we manage in the pilot

  • Legal and privacy functions may conclude that distilled datasets require the same treatment as originals.
  • Regions with different data distributions may be unevenly represented in combined models.

10Pilot proposal

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative), excluding customer legal review time · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One group-level model that currently requires, or is blocked by, transfer of complete regional datasets.
Required inputs
  • Data from two regional environments for the selected task
  • A compute environment in each region
  • Legal and privacy guidance on whether derived datasets may be transferred
  • The target evaluation metric and regional baselines
Existing baseline
Regional models trained locally on full data, or a central model on complete copies where one already exists.
Evaluation methodology
  • Distill in each region and evaluate locally against regional baselines.
  • Where transfer is approved, train a combined model on distilled datasets and compare with the baseline.
  • Measure transfer volume, storage and regional distillation overhead.
Technical success criteria
  • Model performance within customer-defined tolerance of baseline in each region.
  • No region materially under-represented in the combined model.
Economic success criteria
  • Transfer volume and central copies reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Net saving or avoided cost positive after regional overhead.
Deliverables
  • Fidelity report per region and for the combined model
  • Transfer, storage and overhead comparison
  • Documentation of the derivation process to support the customer's own legal assessment
  • Production adoption plan
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • Customer legal and privacy approval of the operating model
  • Named data owners for each region
