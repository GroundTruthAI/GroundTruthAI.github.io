Discuss your AI energy footprint

01Business challenge

AI is now a visible part of enterprise energy demand. Training and retraining models, and storing and moving the data behind them, consume electricity in company data centers and in the cloud regions that serve them. Sustainability teams are asked to report this footprint and to show credible reduction plans.

The usual levers sit outside the AI workload: cleaner power contracts, more efficient facilities and newer hardware. These matter, but they do not change how much computation each model requires. Inside the workload, the main driver is the amount of data processed per training run, multiplied by the number of runs.

Organizations need a lever they can apply workload by workload, and evidence strong enough to survive scrutiny from auditors and stakeholders.

02Business opportunity

A measurable, workload-level reduction in training energy that can feed sustainability reporting once verified.

Where conventional architectures fall short

Renewable power procurement and certificates

Addresses the carbon intensity of electricity, not the amount of computation; the workload consumes the same energy.

Hardware and facility efficiency upgrades

Effective but capital-intensive and slow, and gains can be absorbed by growing data volumes and more frequent retraining.

Smaller or simpler models

Reduces compute but can sacrifice capability; it is a model-design decision, not always available for an existing production model.

Carbon-aware scheduling

Shifts when and where workloads run, which lowers emissions intensity, but does not reduce the energy the workload needs.

Generic architectural approaches, not specific products. How distillation compares with other techniques.

03GroundTruth AI approach

GroundTruth AI reduces the amount of data an eligible training workload processes. Models are trained on a task-specific distilled dataset validated against the full data, so each training run does less computation. Because distillation consumes energy too, every assessment measures net energy: workload energy saved minus the energy of distillation and regeneration.

  1. Select eligible workloads

    Identify training workloads where energy scales with data volume and where accuracy tolerances allow a distilled training set.

  2. Measure the baseline

    Meter energy per training run on the actual hardware or obtain provider-reported figures, together with run counts per year.

  3. Distill and validate

    Generate a distilled dataset and confirm model quality against the full-data baseline.

  4. Measure net energy

    Meter distillation and distilled-data training runs; report net savings per run and per year, distillation included.

  5. Prepare for reporting

    Hand the measurement method and data to the sustainability team for verification before any figure is used in external reporting.

What this does not do

  • It does not make environmental claims on its own; figures require customer-side lifecycle measurement and verification.
  • It does not reduce embodied emissions of hardware or facility overheads directly.
  • Dataset reduction does not translate one-for-one into data center energy reduction.

08Technical considerations

For a pilot, distillation runs as a batch job alongside the selected workload, and energy is metered for baseline training, distillation and distilled training on the same hardware. Cloud workloads may rely on provider-reported energy, which can limit precision; that limitation is documented in the results.

Required infrastructure

  • Access to the hardware or cloud environment where the selected workload trains.
  • A metering method that can isolate energy per training and distillation run.
  • Compute for distillation runs (CPU-first; sizing set in the pilot).

Operational constraints

  • Workload-level energy metering is not available in every environment.
  • Facility overheads (cooling, power conversion) are allocated, not measured per workload.
  • Lifecycle and embodied emissions are outside the measurement scope unless the customer adds them.

To be validated in an evaluation

Open questions

  • No published measurement methodology for the energy figures.
  • Distillation energy has not been reported separately.
  • No data-center-scale or cloud-workload measurement.
  • No lifecycle assessment or third-party verification.

Risks managed in the pilot

  • Net savings can be small if distillation must be re-run frequently.
  • Rebound effect: cheaper training can lead to more training runs, offsetting savings.

Data governance

  • Energy figures shown today are internal measurements. Any environmental claim requires customer-side lifecycle measurement that includes the energy of distillation itself.

09Available evidence

Enterprise application for pilot evaluation — not yet validated in production

Internal measurements indicate lower training energy per run after data reduction on a workstation and on IoT/edge devices. The methodology is not yet published, the figures do not separately report distillation energy, and no data-center or lifecycle measurement exists. No environmental claim should be made until a pilot measures net energy end to end.

What supports it today

  • 86% less energy per training run on a workstation after data reduction (internal measurement; distillation energy not separately reported).
  • 39% less energy on IoT/edge devices at the point of capture (internal measurement).
  • 86–95% data reduction on four public benchmarks with a maximum accuracy change of −2.8 pp.

Enterprise pilot design

Indicative duration: 8–12 weeks (indicative) · Pricing: scoped per engagement, not published.

Customer problem
One recurring training workload whose energy the customer wants to reduce and measure.
Required inputs
  • The workload's training data, pipeline and evaluation metric
  • Run frequency over the past year
  • Access to power telemetry or provider-reported energy for the workload
  • Compute for distillation runs
Existing baseline
Metered energy and cost of the current training run on the full dataset.
Evaluation methodology
  • Meter energy for full-data training, distillation and distilled-data training on the same hardware.
  • Evaluate model quality for both on the same held-out test set.
  • Compute net annual energy and cost using actual run frequency, distillation included.
  • Document measurement boundaries and uncertainty with the sustainability team.
Technical success criteria
  • Model performance within the customer-defined tolerance of baseline.
  • Energy measurements reproducible within an agreed uncertainty.
Economic success criteria
  • Net energy and compute cost per year reduced by a customer-defined target.
  • Saving remains positive after distillation and regeneration energy.
Deliverables
  • Net energy and cost report, distillation included
  • Documented measurement method and boundaries
  • Evaluation report (full vs. distilled)
  • Roll-out plan for further eligible workloads
Production adoption conditions
  • Technical and economic criteria met
  • Measurement method accepted by the sustainability team
  • Verification route agreed before any external use of figures
