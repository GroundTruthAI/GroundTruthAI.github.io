Solution family

AI infrastructure efficiency

Reduce the cost of developing, training and maintaining AI models.

The enterprise problem

Every training and retraining cycle reprocesses growing data histories. Compute, storage, energy and wall-clock time scale with volume, while much of that volume adds little to the model.

The business outcome

Lower data-proportional training and retraining cost and faster experimentation, net of the cost of distillation.

Business cases

4 business cases in this solution family

  • AI training cost optimization

    Train on the data that matters, and stop paying to process the rest.

    Problem

    Training and retraining on ever-larger datasets drives compute spend, slows experimentation and ties up shared infrastructure.

    Outcome

    Lower compute per training run and faster experimentation, within an agreed accuracy tolerance.

    Read the business case

  • Continuous model retraining

    Retrain production models on a validated distilled dataset instead of the full history every cycle.

    Problem

    Production models are retrained on ever-growing full histories, so each cycle costs more compute, time and storage than the last.

    Outcome

    Lower recurring retraining cost and shorter model update cycles, with fidelity checked every cycle.

    Read the business case

  • AI infrastructure and data center efficiency

    Do more AI work per server, per terabyte and per kilowatt-hour.

    Problem

    Enterprise AI workloads consume growing storage, compute and energy, and much of that capacity is spent on redundant training data.

    Outcome

    Better utilization of AI infrastructure for eligible workloads, net of distillation overhead.

    Read the business case

  • Sustainable AI infrastructure

    Cut the energy of eligible AI workloads by processing less data, and prove it with measurements.

    Problem

    AI workloads add energy demand that sustainability teams must account for, but few levers reduce it without reducing AI ambitions.

    Outcome

    A measurable, workload-level reduction in training energy that can feed sustainability reporting once verified.

    Read the business case

What it changes

  • Training efficiency on task-specific datasets
  • Better use of shared compute capacity
  • Lower recurring processing costs
  • Faster experimentation cycles
  • More efficient retraining
  • Potential energy savings, measured per workload

What it does not change

  • Distilled datasets are derived from source data and stay under the same classification, protection and transfer rules.
  • Distillation is not anonymization, encryption or a security control, and grants no legal authorization for any transfer.
  • Original data remains in its source environment under existing retention policies; nothing is deleted by default.

Illustrative enterprise scenario

AI training cost optimization: how it could work

Illustrative enterprise scenario Hypothetical organization; assumptions are for discussion and not contractual.

Retail ML platform team

Enterprise context
A retailer's central ML platform trains demand, pricing and personalization models on several years of transaction and clickstream history.
Current architecture
Each experiment and each retraining run processes the full history on shared compute capacity.
Business problem
Training spend and queue times rise with data volume. Data scientists wait for capacity, and experimentation slows.
Proposed GroundTruth AI architecture
For selected models, GroundTruth AI produces a validated distilled training set. Experiments run on the distilled set; candidate models are validated against the full-data baseline before release.

Illustrative assumptions

  • Annual training compute for the selected models: 400,000 (in any currency)
  • 60% of that spend scales with data volume
  • Assumed dataset reduction: 70%, an illustrative planning value
  • Data-proportional spend reduced by about 168,000 before distillation and licensing costs

Planning values, not measurements. The achievable reduction and model quality are validated per workload.

Technical constraints

  • Accuracy tolerance agreed per model before the evaluation
  • Distilled sets are task-specific; a new target needs regeneration

Potential economic outcomes

  • Lower compute per training run and per experiment
  • Net of the compute used for distillation

Potential strategic outcomes

  • More experiments on the same capacity
  • Faster iteration from idea to validated model

Technical validation requirements

  • Accuracy within tolerance on a full-data test set
  • Measured compute and wall-clock time per run, including distillation
  • How often the distilled set can be reused across experiments
