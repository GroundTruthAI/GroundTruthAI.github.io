Distributed enterprise intelligence

Enable enterprise-wide intelligence without centralizing all your raw data.

The enterprise problem

Plants, subsidiaries, regions and acquired companies keep their data in separate, separately governed environments. Central AI and analytics programs that first require a complete raw copy in one place collide with segregation, ownership and transfer rules, and many initiatives never start.

The business outcome

Central model training and analytics on approved, locally distilled datasets, while original data stays in its authorized environment.

What it changes

  • Analytical collaboration across segregated data domains
  • Centralized model training on approved, locally distilled datasets
  • Source data environments preserved as they are
  • Multi-site industrial intelligence
  • Less dependency on complete data consolidation
  • New analytical initiatives that data architecture would otherwise hold back

What it does not change

  • Distilled datasets are derived from source data and stay under the same classification, protection and transfer rules.
  • Distillation is not anonymization, encryption or a security control, and grants no legal authorization for any transfer.
  • Original data remains in its source environment under existing retention policies; nothing is deleted by default.

Illustrative enterprise scenario

OT/IT data segregation: how it could work

Illustrative enterprise scenario Hypothetical organization; assumptions are for discussion and not contractual.

Global manufacturer, 20 facilities

Enterprise context
A manufacturing group operates 20 industrial facilities. Each facility keeps its operational data in an authorized OT environment, behind its own industrial DMZ.
Current architecture
Plant historians record sensor and equipment data locally. Central analytics receive selected KPIs, but the enterprise AI team has no governed path for training data at scale.
Business problem
The central AI team wants to train predictive-maintenance models on data from all 20 facilities. The conventional approach would replicate about 200 TB of eligible history into enterprise IT, a request that OT security, plant owners and infrastructure budgets all resist.
Proposed GroundTruth AI architecture
A distillation engine runs in each facility's authorized site-operations zone. It builds a task-specific dataset for the predictive-maintenance model, validates it, and, after owner approval, passes it through the existing DMZ transfer path. The central team trains one model on the combined approved datasets.

Illustrative assumptions

  • 20 facilities × 10 TB of eligible training data = 200 TB
  • Assumed dataset reduction: 90%, an illustrative planning value, not a measured result for this workload
  • Optimized data transferred: 20 TB
  • Potential avoided transfer: 180 TB

Planning values, not measurements. The achievable reduction and model quality are validated per workload.

Technical constraints

  • Raw historian data stays in each facility, under existing retention policies
  • Only existing, approved OT/IT exchange mechanisms are used
  • No change to control systems or safety-critical control loops

Potential economic outcomes

  • About 180 TB of raw training copies not transferred, ingested or stored centrally (illustrative)
  • Smaller central training runs, net of source-side distillation compute

Potential strategic outcomes

  • Enterprise-wide predictive maintenance across all facilities
  • Analytical collaboration between plants without a central raw OT data lake
  • A repeatable pattern for the next industrial use case

Technical validation requirements

  • Predictive performance within the agreed tolerance of a full-data baseline
  • Coverage of rare failure modes in every facility's distilled dataset
  • Cross-site generalization, including a facility held out from training
  • Source-side compute and energy overhead measured on site hardware
