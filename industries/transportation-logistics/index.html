Sector context

Rail operators, airlines, ports, fleet operators and logistics networks use AI for predictive maintenance, network planning, energy efficiency and safety. Data is produced by vehicles, signaling systems, terminal equipment and depots spread across regions or countries.

Much operational data sits in safety-relevant or segregated systems, and connectivity from moving assets is intermittent. Central teams therefore receive partial, delayed or aggregated data, which limits fleet-wide models.

Typical data estate

  • Onboard vehicle and rolling-stock telemetry
  • Signaling, traffic management and terminal control data
  • Maintenance and depot records
  • Telematics and GPS traces
  • Operational schedules and event logs

Constraints a solution must respect

Technical

  • Intermittent connectivity from moving assets.
  • Mixed fleets with different sensor sets and data formats.
  • Event-heavy data where rare incidents carry the most value.

Operational

  • Safety-relevant systems with certification and change constraints.
  • Maintenance windows tied to timetables and asset availability.
  • Data often owned by different operators, lessors or manufacturers.

Governance

  • Transport safety regulation and operator security requirements.
  • Critical-infrastructure cybersecurity obligations in many jurisdictions.
  • Location data from vehicles can be personal data when linked to drivers or crew.

Distilled datasets remain subject to the same sector regulation, data protection and security controls as their source data.

A sensible first pilot

Start with a component-failure model for one vehicle or equipment class where depot data is already collected centrally in part.
