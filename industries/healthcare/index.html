Sector context

Hospital groups, payers, research networks and life-sciences companies want analytics across institutions: outcome prediction, operational planning, real-world evidence and research cohorts. The data is held by separate institutions with their own systems, consent regimes and ethics approvals.

Pooling raw patient data is slow and often not permitted. Even where it is, the governance effort per dataset is high, so analytics programs move at the pace of data-sharing agreements.

Typical data estate

  • Electronic health records
  • Laboratory and imaging systems
  • Claims and administrative data
  • Clinical trial and registry data
  • Device and remote-monitoring data

Constraints a solution must respect

Technical

  • Heterogeneous coding systems and data models across institutions.
  • Small, sensitive subpopulations where under-representation creates bias.
  • Mixed structured, imaging and free-text data.

Operational

  • Clinical systems are prioritized over analytics workloads.
  • Ethics, consent and data-access committees review each new use.
  • Limited data engineering capacity in individual institutions.

Governance

  • Health data is special-category personal data under GDPR and protected health information under HIPAA.
  • Distilled datasets derived from patient records are not anonymized by default.
  • Data-sharing agreements and ethics approvals define permitted use per project.

Distilled datasets remain subject to the same sector regulation, data protection and security controls as their source data.

A sensible first pilot

Start with an operational (non-clinical-decision) model, such as capacity or length-of-stay forecasting, across two sites with existing data-sharing approval.
