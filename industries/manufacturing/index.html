Sector context

Manufacturers run AI for predictive maintenance, quality inspection, process optimization and energy management. The data that matters most (sensor histories, machine states, quality measurements) lives in plant historians and control systems, segmented from enterprise IT and often different at every site.

The constraint is rarely a shortage of data. It is the cost and policy friction of moving plant data to where models are trained, the heterogeneity between lines and sites, and the scarcity of labeled failures that predictive models depend on.

Typical data estate

  • Process historians and time-series databases
  • SCADA, PLC and DCS signals
  • MES and quality management records
  • Maintenance work orders (CMMS / EAM)
  • Machine vision and inspection images

Constraints a solution must respect

Technical

  • High-frequency, multivariate time series with site-specific tag naming.
  • Rare, sparsely labeled failure events.
  • Different equipment generations and configurations across lines and plants.

Operational

  • No added load or risk on control systems; availability comes first.
  • Change windows in OT environments are limited and planned well in advance.
  • Plant engineering time for labeling and validation is scarce.

Governance

  • OT security architectures based on zones and conduits, often aligned with IEC 62443.
  • Process data may be trade secret or export-controlled.
  • Equipment vendor contracts can restrict data use or remote access.

Distilled datasets remain subject to the same sector regulation, data protection and security controls as their source data.

A sensible first pilot

Start with one predictive-maintenance model for a well-instrumented asset class at a single plant that already exports historian data to IT.
