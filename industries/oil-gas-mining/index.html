Sector context

Upstream, midstream and mining operators apply AI to equipment reliability, process optimization, safety monitoring and production forecasting. Assets such as offshore platforms, pipelines and remote mine sites generate large volumes of sensor data far from central data centers.

Connectivity from these sites is often satellite or microwave, expensive and limited, and operational networks are tightly segregated for safety reasons. Moving full raw histories to a central platform is slow, costly and frequently against policy.

Typical data estate

  • Process historians on platforms, plants and mine sites
  • Rotating-equipment vibration and condition data
  • Drilling, production and haulage telemetry
  • Maintenance and inspection records
  • Geospatial and survey data

Constraints a solution must respect

Technical

  • Low-bandwidth, high-cost links from remote sites.
  • Harsh-environment hardware with limited compute at site.
  • Heterogeneous equipment across assets acquired over decades.

Operational

  • Safety-critical operations with strict permit-to-work and change control.
  • Limited on-site IT staff; remote support is the norm.
  • Production continuity drives every intervention window.

Governance

  • Segregated control networks under corporate and sector security standards.
  • Joint-venture and partner agreements that govern data ownership and sharing.
  • Host-country rules on data and operational information in some jurisdictions.

Distilled datasets remain subject to the same sector regulation, data protection and security controls as their source data.

A sensible first pilot

Start with rotating-equipment condition data from one asset with an existing historian export and a known history of failures.
